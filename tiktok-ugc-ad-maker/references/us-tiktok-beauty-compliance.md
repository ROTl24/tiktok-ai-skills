# U.S. TikTok Beauty Compliance Reference

Use this reference before drafting U.S.-targeted TikTok beauty, skincare, lip-care, health-adjacent, influencer, or AI-generated UGC ads.

This is a creative compliance guardrail, not legal advice. If the user needs legal clearance, tell them to verify with counsel and product substantiation.

## TikTok Creative Basics

TikTok's Creative Codes emphasize:

- Think TikTok-first: make content feel native to the platform.
- Shoot 9:16 vertical footage and leave screen space for the TikTok UI.
- Use a reliable hook, body, close structure.
- Build with sound: voice, narration, music, and sound effects should support the story.

Practical rule: write the ad as a creator video first, not as a polished brand commercial.

Source: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes

## TikTok Misleading Claims

TikTok ad content and landing pages must not promise or exaggerate product effects. Avoid absolute or guaranteed results such as "works instantly", "best", "permanently", "guaranteed", or "cures".

Before-and-after or product-effect comparisons can be restricted if they give a false or distorted impression. If a comparison is used, keep it honest, typical, supported, and non-medical.

Landing pages must match the ad's product, price, promotion, claim, and CTA. Do not introduce a product in the video and send users to a vague or different page.

Source: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content

## TikTok Branded Content

Branded content includes posts made for payment, gifts, affiliate commissions, promo codes, or other commercial relationships.

For branded content:

- Enable TikTok's commercial content disclosure toggle.
- Make the promoted product clear verbally and/or in the text caption.
- Do not make false, deceptive, or misleading claims.
- Do not exploit body-image insecurities or promote unhealthy beauty ideals.

Source: https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en

## TikTok AIGC Disclosure

If the creator, voice, footage, or primary subject is significantly AI-generated or AI-altered, use TikTok's AIGC label or add a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark, or sticker.

Examples that need caution:

- a fully AI-generated creator
- AI voice or lip-sync making a person say something they did not say
- a real source image altered so the primary subject appears to do something they did not do

Source: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content

## FTC Endorsement Disclosure

For U.S. consumers, a creator must disclose a material connection to a brand. Material connections include payment, free products, discounted products, affiliate commissions, employment, family relationships, or other incentives.

Good disclosure behavior:

- Put the disclosure in the video itself, not only in the description, profile, comments, or a hidden hashtag block.
- Use clear words such as "Ad", "Sponsored", or "Thanks to [brand] for the free product".
- Use the same language as the endorsement.
- If the endorsement is both visual and spoken, disclosure is stronger when both visual and audible.
- Do not claim personal experience with a product the creator has not tried.
- Do not make claims that require proof the advertiser does not have.

Sources:

- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
- https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf

## FDA Cosmetic Claim Boundary

Cosmetic claims must be truthful and not misleading. In the U.S., a cosmetic may be treated as a drug if it is marketed for treating or preventing disease, or for affecting the structure or function of the body, including skin.

For lip-care ads, safer language usually describes appearance, feel, routine, sensory experience, and ingredient presence:

- "feels softer"
- "looks smoother"
- "adds a glossy finish"
- "not sticky on me"
- "contains peptides and oils"
- "strawberry cake scent"

Avoid unless substantiated and legally cleared:

- "heals cracked lips"
- "repairs damaged skin"
- "treats eczema/cold sores"
- "permanently plumps"
- "changes lip structure"
- "clinically proven to cure"
- "replaces medical treatment"

Source: https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/cosmetics-labeling-claims

## Safer Rewrite Examples

| Risky wording | Why risky | Safer rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "repairs damaged lips overnight" | exaggerated result and possible structure/function claim | "my lips look softer and less dry by the next morning" |
| "heals cracked lips" | treatment/healing claim | "helps my lips feel comfortable when they look dry" |
| "permanently plumps your lips" | structure/function and permanence claim | "gives a temporary glossy, fuller-looking finish" |
| "better than Dior/ChapStick" | unsupported competitor comparison | "feels more luxe than the basic balms I usually use" |
| "clinically proven" | requires substantiation | use only if the user provides valid study support |
| "no one needs lip filler after this" | medical/procedure comparison and unrealistic expectation | "for days when I want a soft glossy look without a heavy product" |
